## Summary
This session, hosted by The Outreach Collective (TOC), is an interactive workshop titled "Decoding the Ever-Expanding Cast: Understanding the characters reshaping education." Facilitated by Bhakti and Amrita Ghulati, the session guides college counseling professionals, admissions officers, and education service providers through mapping the diverse ecosystem of stakeholders involved in a student's educational journey—from discovery to on-campus experience. Participants engage in breakout room exercises and polls to analyze roles, identify operational friction points, evaluate incentives, and establish foundational principles for building trust within the higher education landscape.

## Key Topics & Takeaways

### 1. Evolution of the Higher Education Guidance Ecosystem
* Historically, formal career counseling was nearly non-existent for secondary school students; career advice typically came from informal mentors, relatives, or mid-career advisors.
* Today, counseling begins much earlier (middle/high school), encompassing decisions on schools, curricula, test preparation, profile building, financial aid, and career pathways.
* The cast of characters surrounding a student has expanded dramatically, mapping out to over 24 distinct professional roles and service categories.

### 2. The Six Functional Stages of the Student Journey
* **01. Discover:** Initial career/interest exploration (School guidance counselors, psychometric platforms, experiential learning providers, edu-influencers/content creators, mental health counselors).
* **02. Decide:** Narrowing choices down to a shortlist (Independent Educational Consultants [IECs], university regional representatives/outreach teams, student recruitment agents, aggregator/discovery marketplaces).
* **03. Prepare:** Profile development, skill acquisition, and testing (Test prep providers, test administrators like College Board, research/mentorship programs like Polygence/RISE, skilling/portfolio builders, mental health support).
* **04. Apply:** Submitting applications and securing funding (Essay/application specialists, university admissions teams, application SaaS like Cialfo/Unifrog/Univiser, scholarship and funding platforms).
* **05. Transition:** Operational and logistical hurdles between acceptance and matriculation (Visa/compliance advisors, forex and student finance providers, government/trade bodies like Austrade, housing and arrival services, pre-departure mental health support).
* **06. Experience:** On-campus learning, retention, and post-study pathways (University leadership and faculty, student wellbeing/support teams, global immersion providers, alumni networks, employability and career services).

### 3. Stakeholder Alignment, Incentives, and Friction Points
* **Funding & Motivation Alignment:** Evaluating who pays each provider clarifies primary loyalties (e.g., agents paid by institutions per enrollment vs. independent consultants paid by families). Misaligned incentives are a primary source of friction.
* **Key Tensions Identified:**
  * *University Outreach Yield Goals vs. Family Cost Reality:* Identified by 75% of participants as the sharpest point of tension due to currency fluctuations, rising tuition, and increasing scrutiny of ROI in higher education.
  * *School Counselor Capacity vs. External IECs/Agents:* Tensions over student ownership, conflicting advice, and communication boundaries.
  * *Service Platforms Serving the Professional vs. the Student:* B2B vs. B2C user engagement challenges in ed-tech SaaS platforms.

### 4. Rules of Engagement for Building Trust
* **Be Explicit About Financial Incentives:** Transparency regarding who pays commissions or service fees is essential to prevent hidden bias.
* **Respect Hand-Offs & Boundaries:** Never bypass professional referrers (e.g., going directly to parents behind a school counselor’s back).
* **Stay in Your Lane of Expertise:** Clearly define scope of services (counseling, testing, visa support, essay review) and refer out when specialized support is needed.
* **Make Outcomes Legible:** Clearly demonstrate qualitative and quantitative value without making unrealistic guarantees regarding university admissions.
* **Avoid Dismissiveness:** Validate and collaborate with emerging roles rather than dismissing non-traditional providers or technologies (e.g., AI tools, edu-influencers).
