## Summary
This panel discussion, titled "Beyond Black Robes," explores modern career pathways in the legal profession beyond traditional courtroom litigation. Hosted by Anchal Shroff of The Craft Ed Co and co-hosted by Bhakti Shah, the session features admissions and legal education leaders Deepu Krishna (BITS Law School) and Harsh Vardhan Tiwari (O.P. Jindal Global University). The speakers discuss evolving specializations, law school culture, experiential learning opportunities like internships and summer immersions, international legal education versus studying in India, the impact of AI on the profession, and advice for aspiring law students.

## Key Topics & Takeaways

### Evolution of the Legal Landscape
* **Beyond Traditional Litigation:** Law has expanded well beyond courtrooms and black robes into corporate compliance, legal tech, public policy, sports law, intellectual property (IP), entertainment law, data privacy, and alternative dispute resolution (ADR).
* **Law as a Universal Language:** Law is increasingly viewed as a foundational language across commerce, governance, content creation, and startups.

### Law School Archetypes & Student Journeys
* **Type 1 (Proactive / Career-Focused):** Enters law school understanding that law is a profession requiring personal brand building, research, publishing, conferences, and structured internships from early years.
* **Type 2 (Complacent / Brand-Reliant):** Relies heavily on the institution's brand name for placements and requires continuous nudging from career service offices.
* **Type 3 (Late Bloomers):** Realizes the competitive nature of the field by Year 3 and catches up through intense skill-building and specialized internships.
* **First-Year Experience:** The first year is crucial for breaking misconceptions about law, developing a calm and analytical mindset, mastering comprehension/communication skills, and adapting to long lecture hours.

### Preparing High School Students for Law
* **Observational Internships ("Observerships"):** Rather than expecting high schoolers to do legal research, structured shadow/observation periods with junior lawyers, law firms, or parent networks help them understand day-to-day legal realities.
* **Summer Programs & Mooting:** University summer immersions (e.g., JGLS Immandar Summer Programme) and school-level moot court/MUN participation build argumentation, reading, and interpretive skills early.

### Legal Studies in India vs. Overseas (LL.B. vs. LL.M.)
* **Practicing in India:** Pursuing an undergraduate law degree (5-year integrated BA/BBA LL.B.) in India is strongly recommended if the student intends to practice in India, avoiding the mandatory 2-year bridge course required by the Bar Council of India for foreign undergraduate law degrees.
* **Going Abroad:** Doing an undergraduate law degree abroad makes sense primarily if the student intends to immigrate or practice in that foreign jurisdiction (subject to local bar requirements). Otherwise, completing an undergraduate degree in India followed by an overseas LL.M. for specialization (and potential dual-qualification) is the most cost-effective and career-flexible route.

### Impact of Artificial Intelligence on Law
* **Junior Tasks Automated:** AI tools (Harvey, Claude, Lucero) are automating clerical tasks such as case law summaries, basic document review, and initial draft preparation.
* **Human Core Remains Indispensable:** Strategic reasoning, client advisory, empathetic advocacy, courtroom representation, and handling human conflict remain irreplaceable by AI. AI will act as an efficiency tool for skilled lawyers rather than a wholesale replacement.

### Pathways for Non-Practice Law Graduates & Alternative Careers
* **Public Policy & Think Tanks:** Graduates with backgrounds in international relations, public policy, or liberal arts combined with an LL.M. or legal studies can work with policy research organizations (e.g., Vidhi Centre), embassies, museums/foundations, or legislative affairs.
* **Legal Process Outsourcing (LPOs) & Consulting:** Global consulting firms (e.g., Big 4, Bain, BCG) and LPOs employ law graduates for due diligence, regulatory analysis, and cross-border document review.
