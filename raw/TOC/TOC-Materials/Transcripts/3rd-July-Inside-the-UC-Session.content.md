## Summary
This webinar brings together international recruitment advisors from three University of California (UC) campuses—UC Riverside, UC Merced, and UC Santa Cruz—to guide counselors and applicants through the UC admissions landscape. The session covers key structural details of the UC system, application timelines, coursework entry requirements, test-blind policy updates, scholarship opportunities, and practical advice on crafting Personal Insight Questions (PIQs). The event concludes with an interactive Q&A addressing international curricula reporting, transcript specifics, and application evaluation processes.

## Key Topics & Takeaways

### UC System Overview & Campus Facts
- **Campus Distribution:** 10 campuses total across California; 9 offer undergraduate degree programs (UC San Francisco offers graduate/medical programs only).
- **Student Body:** Over 300,000 students enrolled across the UC system.
- **Historical Milestones:** UC Berkeley is the oldest UC campus; UC Davis was originally founded as "the Farm"; UC Merced is the newest research university established in the 21st century (with Carnegie R1 status).
- **Independent Evaluation:** Applications are reviewed independently by each UC campus applied to; admissions decisions from one campus do not impact or communicate with another.

### UC Application Timeline (Undergraduate)
- **August 1:** Application opens for drafting.
- **October 1 – November 30:** Filing/submission window for Fall admission.
- **March:** First-year admission decisions released rolling through the month.
- **May 1:** National Statement of Intent to Register (SIR) deadline for first-year students.
- **July 1 / July 15:** Official final transcripts, AP, and IB exam scores due (only required for enrolled students submitting an SIR).
- **Spring Intake (UC Merced):** UC Merced also accepts Spring applications for international students (open July 1 – July 31).

### Academic History & Curricular Requirements
- **Self-Reported Academic History:** Students report all coursework and grades from grades 9 through 12. Unofficial transcripts should be referenced during application entry.
- **7th and 8th Grade Coursework:** Only advanced-level Mathematics and Languages other than English (LOTE) completed in 7th/8th grade should be reported.
- **12th Grade Courses:** Must be entered with current/anticipated status (`IP` for In Progress or `PL` for Planned).
- **Indian & International Boards (CBSE/ICSE/A-Levels):** 
  - Internal high school grades (9th–12th) are entered under Academic History.
  - Official 10th and 12th external board exam results/predictions are entered under the Test Scores > International Exams section.
  - Course names must match transcript titles exactly.

### Testing & English Language Proficiency
- **Test-Free (Test-Blind) Policy:** ACT and SAT scores are not reviewed or considered for admissions decisions or scholarship allocations.
- **English Proficiency Requirement:** Waived if a student has completed three or more years of instruction at a high school where English is the primary medium of instruction.

### Activities, Awards & Scholarships
- **Activities & Awards:** Allows up to 20 entries across 6 categories. Quality and depth of engagement are prioritized over quantity; applicants should order entries by personal importance and commitment rather than chronologically.
- **Merit Scholarships for Non-Residents / International Students:**
  - *UC Riverside:* Non-Resident Achievement Scholarship up to $89,100 over 4 years based on GPA.
  - *UC Santa Cruz:* Undergraduate Dean's Award up to $25,000/year ($100,000 over 4 years).
  - *UC Merced:* Global Access Program Scholarship of $25,000/year ($100,000 over 4 years) for GPA 3.25+, with additional continuing merit scholarship potential.
- **Application Fees:** $95 USD per campus for international/non-resident applicants ($80 for California residents).

### Personal Insight Questions (PIQs)
- **Format:** Applicants respond to **4 out of 8** prompt questions (maximum 350 words per prompt).
- **Strategy & Content:** All 8 questions hold equal weight. Responses should focus on personal impact, leadership, context, and clear details rather than stylistic creative writing or abstract philosophy.
- **Guidelines:** Avoid acronyms without explanation, do not repeat the prompt, avoid quotes/slang/sarcasm, and do not make responses campus-specific since the same responses are transmitted to all selected campuses.
- **External Links:** No hyperlinks, portfolios, or external URLs (e.g., LinkedIn, websites) are opened or evaluated during the initial UC application review.
